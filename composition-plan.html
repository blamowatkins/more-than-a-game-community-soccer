Series badge — .issue-badge Identify the soccer story as part of the Community Voices editorial series. The badge appears in normal flow after the introduction. Use a relative header and an absolute badge on wider screens. Reserve space so the badge cannot cover the title. The .feature-header padding box is the containing block. Keep z-index auto to avoid unnecessary stacking contexts. Return the badge to normal flow on narrow screens. With CSS disabled, its text remains in source order. Inspect the containing block. Test at 320px, 768px, and 1280px, at 200% zoom, and with a longer title. Confirm that the badge never covers text.
Story navigation — .story-guide Help readers move between the soccer story's sections. Navigation appears before the article in normal flow. Use bounded sticky positioning on wide, tall screens. Grid arranges the navigation beside the article. A planned .story-layout grid wrapper contains the navigation and article and bounds sticky travel. The viewport supplies the scroll threshold. Sticky creates a stacking context using --layer-sticky. Use static positioning on narrow or short viewports so every navigation link remains reachable. Without CSS, navigation remains before the article. Test scrolling at the wrapper's start and end. Check keyboard focus, anchor destinations, 200% zoom, and all required viewport widths. Confirm navigation never covers article content.
Editorial pull quotation — .pull-quote Emphasize a short, properly attributed quotation about soccer's community role. The quotation follows the figure in normal flow, with its attribution visible beside the quotation. On wide screens, use a small negative block margin for controlled overlap into reserved space at the figure's bottom. Keep the quotation in flow. A planned .feature-composition wrapper contains the figure and quotation. Relative positioning with --layer-raised creates the quotation's stacking context. Remove the overlap on narrow screens. Without CSS, the figure, caption, quotation, and attribution remain in logical reading order. Test a longer quotation, doubled paragraphs, 200% zoom, and all required viewport widths. Confirm the overlap covers no image detail, caption, article text, links, or focus indicators.
Feature artwork — .feature-figure img Introduce the community soccer setting with an illustration whose angled edges echo field markings. A responsive rectangular image appears in a figure, followed by its caption and credit. Use a subtle polygon clip-path as an enhancement. Keep meaningful image details inside the visible area. Keep captions, text, and controls outside the clipping. The image remains in normal flow within .feature-figure. Its border box is the clipping reference box. A non-none clip-path creates a stacking context. Guard the chosen clipping declaration with @supports. Without clipping, show the complete rectangular image. Preserve contextual alternative text and the caption. Disable clip-path and compare both states. Test all required viewport widths and 200% zoom. Check image meaning, caption visibility, stacking order, and nearby link interaction.
Soccer-ball illustration — .shape-portrait Introduce the section about participation and volunteering with a soccer-themed illustration. A responsive square image appears before the section's paragraphs in normal flow. Use border-radius for a circular presentation. On wide screens, progressively enhance with a float, shape-outside: circle(50%) border-box, and shape-margin to separate text from the illustration. The story section contains the float using flow-root. The image's border box defines the wrapping shape. Border-radius and shape-outside create no stacking context. On narrow screens or without shape support, keep the image un-floated. Disabling shape-outside alone leaves ordinary rectangular float wrapping. Both states must remain readable. Disable shape-outside and compare wrapping. Test 320px, 768px, 1280px, 200% zoom, and doubled paragraphs. Confirm text clears the float, stays comfortably separated, and returns to full width below the illustration.
Rounded figure frame — .feature-figure Visually group the feature artwork, caption, and credit as one editorial figure. The figure is a normal-flow block containing a responsive image and its caption. Use the design system's border-radius token to soften the frame's corners. Keep padding around its contents and reserve bottom space for the planned quotation overlap. The figure remains in its article section. Border-radius creates no stacking context. Remove unnecessary positioning from the starter figure. Without border-radius, the frame has square corners. Keep overflow visible so captions and focus outlines are not clipped by the frame. Disable border-radius and confirm content remains intact. Test long captions, credits, 200% zoom, and all required viewport widths. Confirm quotation overlap uses only reserved space.
